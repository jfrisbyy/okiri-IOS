Why the hard-rejection approach is wrong (for Okiri)
1️⃣ Learners don’t produce “valid sentences”

Especially in:

speech

early writing

emotional expression

“je veux pas ne rien avoir”
is not garbage — it’s a signal:

confusion about negation

confusion about subjunctive / infinitive

interference from English logic

Throwing that away is pedagogical malpractice.

A human teacher would never say:

“This is invalid. Moving on.”

They’d say:

“Ah — you’re mixing negation forms. Let’s fix that.”

2️⃣ Rule-based invalidation doesn’t scale linguistically

Those rules:

double negation detection

word count minimums

pattern banning

Will absolutely:

break in informal French

break in spoken registers

break in other languages (Spanish, Portuguese, Japanese)

You’ll spend forever patching rules instead of teaching.

3️⃣ It contradicts your core promise

Your promise is essentially:

“Okiri understands you even when you don’t understand the language yet.”

A hard INVALID state says the opposite.

The correct middle ground (this is the fix)

You don’t want:

❌ “Generate questions no matter what”

❌ “Reject everything that’s messy”

You want:

Intent-First Gap Resolution
The right architecture (aligned with your vision)
Step 1: Treat raw output as a learner signal, not a sentence

Rename it internally:

❌ sentence

✅ learner_attempt

This reframing matters a LOT.

Step 2: AI infers what concept is broken

Instead of asking:

“Is this sentence valid French?”

Ask:

“What linguistic concepts does this attempt reveal confusion about?”

For:

je veux pas ne rien avoir

The AI should infer:

Negation stacking

Verb complementation after vouloir

English → French transfer error

That’s gold, not trash.

Step 3: Resolve to a canonical teaching target

From the learner attempt, the AI generates:

Canonical targets (examples):

“Negation with ne…pas vs ne…rien”

“Using vouloir + infinitive”

“Expressing ‘I don’t want anything’ in French”

This is what lessons are built from — not the raw sentence.

Step 4: Generate lessons from the concept, not the attempt

Now the question types make sense again.

Example curriculum generated:

Concept: French negation with ‘rien’

Valid questions:

Sentence completion

Error correction

Meaning

Production

Using clean, native sentences, not the broken one.

The original learner attempt is only used as:

diagnostic input

explanation reference (“You said X because…”)

Behavior:

This preserves:

The rule you actually want (write this down)

If learner output is unstable, do not discard it — abstract it.

That’s the difference between:

What to tell your devs (very important)

Here’s the exact correction I’d give them:

Hard rejection is correct for question generation, but not for gap handling.

Invalid learner output should not be dropped — it should be abstracted into a teachable concept, and lessons should be generated from clean, native examples of that concept.

Raw learner attempts are diagnostics, not lesson sources.
