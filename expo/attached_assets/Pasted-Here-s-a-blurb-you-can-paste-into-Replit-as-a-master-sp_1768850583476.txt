You are an AI engineer and learning designer.  
Design the **full conversational learning path for Okiri – French**, integrating:

- Foundation Lessons  
- Read tab (115 regional articles)  
- Speak tab (prompts + real-time feedback)  
- My Deck (gaps SRS)  
- Pronunciation checking  
- Grammar feedback  

The goal is to take an English speaker from zero/low French to **conversational (strong A2 → early B1)**, using Okiri’s “learn from your gaps” core.

## 1. Overall Learning Path

Define a 4‑phase path:

1. Module 1 – Sounds & Survival Basics (A1)  
2. Module 2 – Everyday Life & Routines (A2)  
3. Module 3 – Past, Future & Opinions (A2→B1)  
4. Module 4 – Stories, Problems & Real Conversations (early B1)  

Rules:

- Modules are **finite**, with visible progress and clear “You’re ready for the next phase” check.  
- Reading, Speaking, Deck, and Pronunciation are **hooked into every module**, not separate silos.  
- Any vocabulary, grammar, or pronunciation issue is treated as a **gap** and sent to the Deck for spaced repetition.

***

## 2. Module 1 – Sounds & Survival Basics (A1)

### 2.1 Core Outcomes

By the end of Module 1, the learner can:

- Pronounce key French sounds and phrases so they’re understandable.  
- Introduce themselves and ask/answer basic personal questions.  
- Handle simple interactions in cafés/shops and ask for basic information.  
- Express simple likes/dislikes and wants.

### 2.2 What users actually do

For each lesson in Module 1:

1. **Learn phrases & sounds (Foundation + Pronunciation Check)**  
   - See/hear core phrases (Bonjour, Je m’appelle…, Je viens de…, Je voudrais…, etc.).  
   - Play **ElevenLabs** audio for each phrase.  
   - Press mic → say phrase → system:
     - Uses Web Speech / STT to transcribe.  
     - Compares to target phrase.  
     - Gives simple feedback:  
       - “Clear ✅”  
       - “Understandable, but watch this sound: [u] vs [ou]”  
       - “Hard to understand, listen again and try with this tip.”  
   - If they fail twice on a phrase or specific sound (nasals, French r, liaison), auto-create a **Pronunciation Gap** in the Deck with:  
     - Target phrase  
     - Slow audio  
     - Syllable breakdown + 1‑line tip  

2. **Short Reading (Read tab – beginner content)**  
   - For Module 1, surface **Beginner / Easy** dialogues and micro-texts that match the lesson topic (self‑intro, café, directions).  
   - User reads 3–10 line dialogues with tap‑to‑translate:  
     - Tap word/phrase → definition + translation + audio.  
     - “Add to Deck” button → creates vocab gap.  
   - Lesson rule: learner must complete at least one short reading per lesson (to anchor phrases in context).

3. **Guided Speaking (Speak tab – structured prompts)**  
   - The Speak screen shows **pre‑set prompts** tied to the lesson:  
     - “Introduce yourself”  
     - “Order a drink and something to eat”  
   - Session flow:  
     - Choose 5‑minute “guided” mode.  
     - See 2–3 sub‑prompts as scaffolding.  
     - Speak freely → real-time transcription → grammar checker (LanguageTool) cleans up basic conjugations/gender/errors and shows:  
       - Your sentence  
       - Suggested improved sentence  
       - Short explanation + when your version *would* be correct  
     - Any repeated grammar error or unknown word can be “Add to Deck”.

4. **Mini Writing task**  
   - Write 2–4 sentences (e.g., “Introduce yourself”, “Write a simple café order”).  
   - AI gives fluency suggestions + grammar explanation.  
   - Errors can be added as **Grammar Gaps** to the Deck.

5. **Checkpoint at end of Module 1**  
   - Short speaking check:  
     - Prompt 1: introduce yourself.  
     - Prompt 2: order in a café.  
   - Reviewer logic: “Understandable with basic phrases?” → if yes, unlock Module 2; if not, suggest repeating key lessons.

## 3. Module 2 – Everyday Life & Routines (A2)

### 3.1 Core Outcomes

By the end of Module 2, the learner can:

- Talk about daily routine, work/study, and free time.  
- Make simple plans with others (when/where/what).  
- Talk about likes/dislikes with short reasons.  
- Handle common transactions (transport, supermarket, basic travel).

### 3.2 What users actually do

For each lesson in Module 2:

1. **Foundation Lesson – Target function**  
   - Focus topics:  
     - Daily routine verbs in present.  
     - Hobbies and frequency adverbs.  
     - Making plans (“On se voit…?”, “Tu veux…?”).  
   - Interactive explanation + pattern drills.

2. **Pronunciation-in-context**  
   - Less drilling than Module 1, but still:  
     - Listen & repeat key sentences.  
     - Check pronunciation for problem clusters (liaison, reduction).  
   - Pronunciation gaps continue to feed Deck.

3. **Reading – Everyday content (Read tab)**  
   - Surface **Beginner/Easy** articles related to:  
     - A typical day (France, Quebec, Senegal, etc.).  
     - How people spend weekends in different regions.  
   - User must:  
     - Read 1–2 pieces per lesson.  
     - Use tap‑to‑translate for unknown words/phrases.  
     - Add at least a few new items to the Deck.  
   - Progress metrics:  
     - % of article read without taps.  
     - Number of gaps created.

4. **Guided Speaking – Routine & Plans**  
   - Speak prompts tied to each lesson, e.g.:  
     - “Describe your weekday.”  
     - “Talk about your hobbies and how often you do them.”  
     - “Make weekend plans with a friend.”  
   - System:  
     - Transcribes speech.  
     - Grammar checks present tense and basic questions.  
     - Shows corrections with brief explanation.  
     - Lets user send recurring conjugation errors to Deck as **Grammar Gap Cards**.

5. **Writing – Short structured texts**  
   - Tasks like:  
     - “Write about a typical Saturday.”  
     - “Write a message suggesting plans with a friend.”  
   - AI tweaks phrasing, highlights a few key corrections, and offers “Add to Deck”.

6. **Checkpoint at end of Module 2**  
   - Speaking: 5–7 minute total over 2–3 prompts (daily routine, hobbies, making plans).  
   - Criteria: can sustain simple conversation with pauses on familiar topics → if yes, unlock Module 3.

***

## 4. Module 3 – Past, Future & Opinions (A2→B1)

### 4.1 Core Outcomes

By the end of Module 3, the learner can:

- Talk about what they did in the past (weekend, trip, life events) using passé composé for common verbs.  
- Talk about future plans and goals (futur proche).  
- Express preferences and simple opinions with reasons.  
- Compare things (cities, jobs, hobbies).

### 4.2 What users actually do

Per lesson in Module 3:

1. **Foundation – Grammar & chunks**  
   - Focus each lesson on functions:  
     - Lesson type A: “Talking about last weekend” (passé composé).  
     - Lesson type B: “Talking about plans” (futur proche).  
     - Lesson type C: “Giving opinions and reasons”.  
   - Introduce high-frequency patterns as chunks, not just tables.

2. **Reading – Past & future stories (Read tab)**  
   - Surface **Easy/Medium** content from multiple regions about:  
     - Someone’s weekend, a trip, typical holidays.  
     - Plans for upcoming events.  
   - User:  
     - Reads, taps unknown items, adds to Deck.  
     - Starts to see more natural, slightly longer texts (2–5 paragraphs).

3. **Speaking – Stories & plans**  
   - Speak prompts like:  
     - “Tell the story of your last weekend.”  
     - “Describe an important memory.”  
     - “Explain your plans for the next 3 months.”  
   - System:  
     - Transcribe.  
     - Grammar check for tense choice and agreement.  
     - Highlight misused tenses or missing auxiliaries; offer corrected sentence and explanation.  
     - Allow one‑tap “Add this error as a gap”.

4. **Writing – Narrative and opinion**  
   - Tasks like:  
     - “Write a short story about a trip.”  
     - “Write about your favorite place and why you like it.”  
   - Same AI feedback → Deck integration.

5. **Pronunciation – Storytelling rhythm**  
   - Short shadowing exercises with slightly longer audio:  
     - Learner listens to a story excerpt.  
     - Shadows (repeats with Audio).  
     - Optionally check a selected sentence for pronunciation quality.

6. **Checkpoint at end of Module 3**  
   - Speaking exam style:  
     - 3 prompts (past, future, opinion).  
     - 8–10 total minutes.  
   - Assessment logic:  
     - Can they narrate events with some coherence?  
     - Can they describe future plans and explain why?  
   - On passing: mark them “almost conversational; ready for real conversation training” and unlock Module 4.

***

## 5. Module 4 – Stories, Problems & Real Conversations (Early B1)

### 5.1 Core Outcomes

By the end of Module 4, the learner can:

- Tell short stories with beginning–middle–end.  
- Explain everyday problems and ask for help.  
- Participate in small talk and basic discussions, reacting to another person.  
- Manage breakdowns: ask for repetition, clarification, rephrase.

### 5.2 What users actually do

Per lesson in Module 4:

1. **Foundation – Conversation strategies**  
   - Teach conversation phrases:  
     - Clarifying: “Comment ?”, “Vous pouvez répéter ?”, “Qu’est-ce que ça veut dire ?”.  
     - Reformulating: “Tu veux dire que…?”, “En fait…”.  
     - Politeness strategies for asking/complaining.  

2. **Reading – Richer, regional content (Read tab)**  
   - Surface **Medium** content across your 12 francophone regions (travel stories, cultural anecdotes, everyday problems).  
   - Encourage reading for gist and pleasure; still allow tap‑to‑translate + Deck.

3. **Speaking – Real conversation simulations (Speak tab)**  
   - Prompts like:  
     - “Tell about a big mistake you made and what you learned.”  
     - “Explain a problem at a hotel or restaurant and ask for a solution.”  
     - “Discuss pros and cons of living in your city.”  
   - System:  
     - Real-time transcription.  
     - Grammar + fluency suggestions.  
     - Encourage use of conversation management phrases.  
     - Recurring breakdowns become Deck gaps.

4. **Writing – Problem & opinion messages**  
   - Tasks like:  
     - “Write an email complaining about a booking problem.”  
     - “Write a post explaining why you prefer X to Y.”  

5. **Pronunciation – Fine-tuning (optional)**  
   - Optional phrase practice on the user’s own typical sentences.  
   - Pronunciation check used more as refinement than gate.

6. **Final Conversational Check & Unlock**

Design a final “conversational exam”:

- 3–4 speaking prompts mixing identity, daily life, stories, problems, and opinions.  
- Total target speaking time: ~15 minutes.  
- Criteria for passing:  
  - Can handle common situations and familiar topics.  
  - Can tell simple stories and explain opinions.  
  - Errors exist but don’t block understanding.

On passing:

- Show a big “You’re now conversational in French” badge.  
- Unlock **full free‑reign mode** in:

  - Speak tab: open prompts, free mode always available.  
  - Read tab: no “foundation required” flags; they can dive anywhere.  

- Messaging: “From now on, Okiri will mostly teach you from your own reading, speaking, and writing. Everything you can’t say or understand becomes your curriculum.”

***

## 6. How to Use Okiri’s Core in Every Phase

Summarize core logic:

- **Read**: always available, but during modules it surfaces texts matched to the current module’s topics/difficulty; tap‑to‑translate creates vocab/phrase gaps.  
- **Speak**: in early modules, shows guided prompts tied to lessons; later, opens up to free prompts; always uses transcription + grammar feedback, with error → Deck.  
- **Deck**: central hub for all gaps: vocab, phrases, grammar, pronunciation; spaced repetition with audio and example sentences.  
- **Pronunciation**: required for early core phrases; optional refinement later; pronunciation gaps are stored and reviewed like vocabulary.  

This is the complete learning pathway from zero to conversational French, fully integrated with Okiri’s adaptive gap engine, reading library, speaking tools, and pronunciation checks.
